Every serial moves through a known set of states

A panel that arrived on the 4th, sat in the warehouse for nine days and was installed on the 13th is a warranty question waiting to happen. A serial is booked in as available, moves to transfer when it changes warehouse and to sold the moment it is dispatched or installed — and any correction sends it back to available rather than leaving a gap in the trail.

  • Imported at goods receipt with warranty and guarantee dates
  • Warehouse-to-warehouse moves marked as transfer
  • Dispatch or installation marks the serial sold
  • Removing a line or a challan frees it again

Two balances, a ledger behind each

“How much do we hold?” and “How much has this installation taken?” are different questions, and a healthy warehouse can still leave a project short. So the ERP keeps two running balances — central warehouse, and project or installer — and every single change appends a credit or a debit row saying what moved, when, and why.

  • Warehouse balance for every item in every store
  • Project and installer balance for what is allocated
  • Each movement recorded as a credit or a debit
  • Nothing recomputed overnight — the history is the truth

The rules the stock engine enforces

A ledger is only trustworthy if the arithmetic cannot drift. These are the checks that run behind every screen — they are why a challan can never quietly push a warehouse negative, and why a deleted challan leaves the same trail a created one did.

  • A challan is refused when the source warehouse is short
  • Editing a line reverses the old quantity, then applies the new one
  • Deleting a challan returns the stock and frees the serials
  • Serials are accepted only when the count matches the line quantity
  • A physical count becomes the new balance, with the difference recorded
  • A return takes quantity off the project and credits the warehouse
